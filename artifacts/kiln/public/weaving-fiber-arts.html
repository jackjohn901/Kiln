Weaving techniques on Kiln

  • Floor loom weaving — large-scale structural textiles, functional cloth, yardage for garments, and wall hangings on 4-shaft and 8-shaft looms
  • Tapestry weaving — image-based woven pictures, pictorial panels, and abstract tapestry work
  • Rigid heddle — a beginner-friendly format producing scarves, wraps, and simple cloth with surprising design potential
  • Backstrap loom — traditional indigenous weaving techniques from Central and South America, producing intricate patterned cloth
  • Natural dyeing — coloring fiber and cloth using plant-based dye sources: madder, weld, Japanese indigo, woad, and more
  • Spinning — converting raw fiber (wool, flax, silk) into yarn before weaving

Process videos — watch weavers at work

One of the most compelling types of craft process content on Kiln is weaving. Warping a floor loom — threading hundreds of ends through heddles and reed — is deeply satisfying to watch. The gradual building of a weave structure, the interplay of color, the physical rhythm of the shuttle — weavers on Kiln share all of it. Follow individual weavers, join the Weaving Guild, and see behind-the-scenes of major tapestry and textile projects.

What is natural dyeing?

Natural dyeing is the process of coloring fiber, yarn, or cloth using plant-based dye sources — roots, flowers, leaves, bark, and other botanical materials. Common dye plants include madder (reds and oranges), weld (yellows), Japanese indigo and woad (blues), and black walnut (browns and grays). Natural dyers on Kiln share their processes in detail — mordanting, dyebath preparation, and the slow chemistry of plant color.
