Pottery Classes & Ceramics Workshops

Learn pottery and ceramics directly from working artists on Kiln. Every class is taught by a maker who actually fires a kiln — not a corporate instructor. Book in-person workshops at artists' studios or join online sessions from anywhere in the world.

Types of ceramics classes available on Kiln

  • Wheel throwing — beginner through advanced, functional and sculptural forms
  • Hand-building — coiling, slab work, pinch pots, sculptural techniques
  • Raku firing — the dramatic reduction firing technique, workshops at artists' studios
  • Glaze chemistry — understanding how glazes work, mixing your own
  • Porcelain — working with fine white clay, translucency, and precision
  • Stoneware functional ware — bowls, mugs, plates, pitchers, jars
  • Sculptural ceramics — figurative and abstract ceramic sculpture
  • Natural ash glazes and salt firing — atmospheric firing techniques
  • Kiln loading and firing — understanding electric, gas, and wood kilns

Why learn from artists on Kiln vs. traditional ceramics schools

When you take a class through Kiln, you learn from someone who has spent years or decades mastering their practice. You see their studio, understand their aesthetic, watch their process videos before you arrive, and buy a piece of their work when you leave. It is a fundamentally different experience from a community college ceramics class.

Many Kiln artists also post their teaching process on their video feed — so you can follow along between sessions, ask questions in the guild community, and continue your learning after the workshop ends.

How to find pottery classes near you

On the Kiln workshops page, filter by technique (ceramics, pottery) and location. You can also browse the Studio Map to find potters who have open studios near you. Many artists offer drop-in studio sessions as well as structured multi-week courses.

Also on Kiln: ceramics process videos

Kiln's main feed is full of ceramics process content — wheel-throwing sessions, trimming, glazing, kiln loading, and the satisfying reveal of a finished firing. You can follow specific potters, join the Ceramics guild, and discover new makers before you decide who to learn from.
