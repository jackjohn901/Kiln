For your app — especially because you’re targeting the craft, glass, artist-process, and creator economy world — the biggest mistake would be launching like a normal tech startup.

You do not need millions of users first.

You need:

1. A passionate niche
2. Daily content creation
3. Artists making money
4. A culture people want to belong to

That’s how you become the “TikTok for art process” instead of just another app.

Phase 1 — Don’t Launch to Everyone

Launch to a tribe first.

Your best opening niche is:

* Glass artists
* Ceramic artists
* Woodworkers
* Metal artists
* Fiber artists
* Craft schools
* Process creators

This is actually GOOD because:

* They create visually addictive content
* They already film process
* They’re underserved
* They share communities
* They travel to the same events

You already have credibility here through Habatat Galleries and your network.

That is a massive advantage.

⸻

The #1 Thing You Need:

Daily addictive process videos

Not polished.
Not perfect.

You need:

* Hot glass
* Kilns opening
* Ceramics breaking
* Sculptures being built
* Failures
* Time lapses
* Packing artwork
* Studio tours
* Before/after
* Artists talking honestly

People LOVE process.

The app must become:

“I can’t stop watching artists create.”

That’s the hook.

⸻

Your Real Competitor

Not Instagram.

Not TikTok.

Your competitor is:

boredom.

You must make art feel:

* exciting
* emotional
* relaxing
* cinematic
* satisfying

⸻

What Gets Apps Off the Ground

1. Seed 50–100 Core Creators

This is EVERYTHING.

Do not launch empty.

You personally recruit:

* known glass artists
* ceramic artists
* craft schools
* younger process creators
* art influencers
* museum educators
* studio assistants

Give them:

* verified badges
* featured placement
* founder status
* early monetization
* free promotion

You need creators posting BEFORE launch.

⸻

2. Build a “Founding Artist” Program

This is critical.

People LOVE exclusivity.

Examples:

* “Founding 100 Artists”
* “Original Kiln Creators”
* “Craft Vanguard”
* “First Flame Artists”

Benefits:

* permanent badge
* boosted algorithm reach
* future monetization perks
* early profile verification
* invitation-only events

This creates identity.

⸻

3. Launch at Real Events

This is where you have an advantage most startups don’t.

You already know:

* collectors
* museums
* artists
* fairs
* schools
* galleries

You should launch physically at:

* Pilchuck Glass School
* Penland School of Craft
* UrbanGlass
* Pittsburgh Glass Center
* SOFA Chicago
* Glass Art Society
* craft fairs
* residencies
* university programs

Set up:

* creator booths
* filming stations
* live uploads
* contests
* creator interviews

Make the app feel alive.

⸻

4. You Need ONE Viral Loop

The easiest:

“Watch this become art.”

That’s the loop.

Examples:

* clay to sculpture
* furnace to vessel
* sketch to finished work
* wood block to furniture
* molten glass to masterpiece

People watch transformation endlessly.

⸻

5. Your Marketing Should NOT Feel Corporate

Big mistake most apps make.

Your app should feel:

* human
* emotional
* artistic
* creator-first
* raw
* authentic

Less:

“Join our platform”

More:

“Show the world how you create.”

⸻

6. Build the Collector Side Later

Do NOT start with heavy sales.

Start with:

* creators
* viewers
* process
* community
* comments
* reposts

Then add:

* commissions
* storefronts
* auctions
* subscriptions
* collector follows

Community first.
Commerce second.

⸻

Art schools and craft centers

Partner with:

* RISD

8. Micro Influencers > Celebrities

Don’t chase giant influencers first.

Find:

* ceramic TikTok creators
* satisfying-process creators
* glassblowers
* wheel throwers
* welders
* woodturners

Even creators with:

* 20k followers

can massively help.

Especially if they post daily.

⸻

9. Your First Goal

Not revenue.

Not investors.

Not millions.

Your first goal:

“Can we get creators addicted to posting?”

If artists post daily:
you win.

If viewers return daily:
you REALLY win.
